## Elsa, Impact

### Tools (4/4)

## Dara, Demand

`demand` · effort `medium`

Owns the demand side. Daily consumption is a variable that moves, not a fixed number.

### Tools (3/3)

- **`get_demand_signal`**(material)
  <br>Customer demand changes not yet reflected in the production schedule.
- **`get_production_schedule`**(material, plant)
  <br>Production schedule and daily consumption rate for one material at one plant.
- **`forecast_demand`**(material, plant, horizon_days?, confidence?)
  <br>Forecast daily consumption for a material at a plant from its withdrawal history, with a prediction interval. Compares the result against the planned rate in master data, which is the figure every cover date in the system is currently divided by. Deterministic: Holt's linear trend over a deseasonalised series, no model. Refuses when the history is too short to say anything.

## Iris, Stock Validity

- **`assess_transfer`**(material, from_plant, to_plant, qty, floor_days?)
  <br>Judge one proposed inter-plant transfer from both ends. The receiving plant is easy to model and the donor is the one that gets forgotten. Returns whether the donor keeps its own safety floor, and when it would empty if it does not. A transfer that breaks the sender is not a solution.

## Clint, Sourcing

`sourcing` · effort `medium`

Finds and qualifies replacement supply: other suppliers, internal stock, fast freight, and substitute materials with whether each is approved for the product.

### Tools (3/3)

- **`find_alternate_sources`**(material)
  <br>Replacement supply options: another supplier, internal stock, or expedited freight.
- **`find_internal_transfers`**(material, to_plant, need_qty, floor_days?)
  <br>Plants that could send a material to one that is short, with what each transfer would cost the plant sending it. Returns the ones that break the donor as well as the ones that do not, because an option removed silently cannot be argued with.
- **`find_substitute_materials`**(material, finished_good?)
  <br>Other materials a finished good may be made from instead of this one, whether each is approved for that product, and how much of the substitute is in stock where. Reads the alternative items in SAP's bill of materials and any uploaded MaterialSubstitute list (engineering's approvals). A substitute not approved for the product comes back with what approving it would take: it cannot be used before then, however much of it is on the shelf.

## Prita, Procurement

`procurement` · effort `medium`

Owns the supplier book as a whole: who the company buys from, in what volume, from which country, on what certificate, and whether each of them kept the date on its own purchase orders. Reads the quotations on file, compares them on price against lead time, and drafts the purchase order that follows from the comparison, with the reference rate for a price quoted in another currency.

### Tools (6/6)

- **`get_supplier_master`**(no arguments)
  <br>The supplier book: every supplier on the approved list, with its country and its certified local-content percentage, plus how many purchase orders reach it and what share of the total volume that is. The suppliers holding no orders come back too, because qualified capacity nobody is using is a finding rather than a hole in the data. Records naming the same company twice are reported through duplicate_of rather than merged, since merging them would be a guess.
- **`get_supplier_performance`**(supplier?)
  <br>How each supplier kept the delivery date on its own purchase orders: how many slipped, the average and the worst slip, what share of the book it carries, and the order it was worst on. Slip is the revised date minus the date on the order, so a supplier that renegotiates its own date forward is late even while the status still reads in transit. Pass a supplier id to ask about one supplier.
- **`get_quotations`**(material?)
  <br>The quotation file in full: material, firm, price per base unit, quoted lead time, and the day the offer stops being valid. Read it to see which materials still have firms bidding and which have run out of live offers. Pass a material code to ask about one; the whole file is long, so narrow it before quoting figures from it. QtnLifecycleStatus comes back as stored and is not a verdict: validity is read from the date beside it. Sorted by material and then by price, so the cheapest offer for a material is the first row of its group.
- **`compare_quotations`**(material, qty)
  <br>Compare the supplier quotations on file for one material: price per base unit, quoted lead time, the total for the quantity you name, and the local-content certificate each firm carries. Ranked by total cost, and it names the fastest offer when that is a different firm, because they usually are and the gap between them is the decision. Offers past their validity date are listed but not ranked, and QtnLifecycleStatus is not taken at face value: it records what the round was closed out as, not what is true on the as-of date.
- **`propose_purchase_order`**(material, supplier, qty, needed_by, rationale)
  <br>Draft a purchase order against a quotation on file and raise it for approval. The price is taken from the quotation and never from the request: a draft cannot quote a number nobody sent us. Refuses when that offer has passed its validity date, naming the firms still quoting instead. Returns the draft with its total, its lead time and the certificate behind it, plus, when this is called from a run on a signal, the id of the action now waiting in the approval queue. Nothing is sent to SAP and nothing is approved here: a purchase order always waits for a person.
- **`get_exchange_rate`**(base, quote?)
  <br>The latest European Central Bank reference rate between two currencies: how many `quote` one `base` buys, and the date the rate was set. Use it to put a supplier's price in a foreign currency next to a rupiah one. A reference rate, not the rate a bank deals at. The ECB publishes no VND.

## Milo, Logistics

`logistics` · effort `medium`

Models arrival dates that survive contact with reality: port dwell measured on past customs releases, where an import stands in customs and why it is held, transhipment, transport mode. Reads the outside world for the lane: the weather and hazards at a port, the news, and the holidays at either end.

### Tools (7/7)

- **`get_shipment_status`**(purchase_order)
  <br>Position and status of a shipment in transit.
- **`estimate_eta`**(option_id, dwell_days?, port?)
  <br>Estimate a realistic arrival date for a supply option: its booked arrival plus port dwell and customs clearance when it is imported. The dwell is the median measured on the customs declarations released at the port, with the 80th percentile as the slow case; pass dwell_days only to override it. With no customs history it falls back to the rule pack's default and says the date rests on that assumption. Deterministic, not a model guess.
- **`get_customs_status`**(purchase_order?, port?)
  <br>Where an import stands in customs: its declaration, the lane it was given (green, yellow, red, MITA), whether it is released, under inspection or held, why, and how many days it has sat at the port, beside how long releases on that lane usually take. Pass a purchase order, or a port to list every declaration still open there. Customs data is not in SAP S/4HANA: it comes from an uploaded CustomsDeclaration file, and is missing when none is on record.
- **`get_port_conditions`**(port)
  <br>Live conditions at a port or plant: the next three days of forecast wind gusts, rain and (for ports) wave height from Open-Meteo, each day flagged where it crosses a named threshold (gale gusts 62 km/h, waves 4 m, rain 100 mm), plus any current cyclone, flood, quake, eruption, fire or drought within reach from GDACS, NASA EONET and USGS. Takes a UN/LOCODE such as CNNGB or a facility code such as KRW1.
- **`search_news`**(query, days?)
  <br>Recent news headlines from Google News for a query, newest first: headline, publisher, time and link. Headlines only, the articles are not read. Use it to find out whether a port closure, strike or storm is being reported. A headline is someone else's claim: cite it as reported, never as fact.
- **`get_public_holidays`**(country, start?, end?)
  <br>National public holidays in one country between two dates, from Nager.Date: when a supplier, a port's customs office or a trucking market closes. Covers ID, CN, JP, KR, VN, SG, HK and most others; not MY, TH or TW. Indonesia's list leaves out cuti bersama (collective leave).
- **`web_brief`**(question)
  <br>Ask a web-search model one question about the outside world (is a port open, has a carrier suspended a service) and get a short answer with the pages it read. Model-written: use it to find where to look and cite its links; take no number or date from it that a cited page does not state. Costs a model call.

## Kira, Rules

`compliance` · effort `high` · **has veto: a rejection here cannot be overturned on cost**

Tests every candidate against TKDN and the contract floor, LARTAS licensing for a new origin, and the holiday calendar, from the rule pack, and says which rules are verified and which are assumptions. Her rejection cannot be overturned on cost.

### Tools (3/3)

- **`check_local_constraints`**(option_id)
  <br>Test one supply option against TKDN, LARTAS and the holiday calendar. Returns allowed/blocked with a reason that names the rule and the numbers, the rules it applied and where each came from. A rejection here is final and must not be overridden on cost.
- **`check_supplier_constraints`**(supplier)
  <br>Test one SUPPLIER against TKDN and LARTAS, for a purchase that comes from a quotation rather than from a costed supply option. Returns allowed/blocked with a reason that names the rule and the numbers, the rules it applied and where each came from. A rejection here is final and must not be overridden on price. Use it before drafting a purchase order; use check_local_constraints for an option the sourcing search returned.
- **`get_rule_pack`**(no arguments)
  <br>The rules check_local_constraints applies, each with its value, the document it was read from and whether a person verified it there: the TKDN contract floor and the current position, the LARTAS licensing delay for a new origin country, the default port dwell, the collective leave on file and how many TKDN certificates are held. Read it to cite a rule, or to say plainly that one is an assumption.

## Tara, Costing

`simulation` · effort `medium`

Costs the options that cleared the rules, and their combinations, and when the material cannot cover every customer order, which orders to serve first and what the late ones pay. Deterministic: she calls engine/simulate.py and engine/allocate.py rather than reasoning it out.

### Tools (2/2)

- **`simulate_scenario`**(material, plant, allowed_options?)
  <br>Compute cost, stockout date, OTIF, and TKDN impact for every supply option that cleared the rules, plus their combinations. Also returns compliance_cost: what obeying the rules cost, when an option that was struck out would have been cheaper, and which rule struck it. Deterministic, the same inputs always give the same answer. Refuses to emit figures when the inputs are not trustworthy.
- **`allocate_short_supply`**(material, plant?, available_qty?, supply_returns?, days_to_ship?)
  <br>When a material cannot cover every customer order before supply returns, decide which orders get what is left. The orders whose lateness costs the most contract penalty per unit of material are served first and the rest wait for the new supply. Returns the orders to serve, in the order to build them, the ones deferred with their days late and penalty, and the penalty total set against serving them in due-date order. Deterministic, engine/allocate.py. Without penalty terms on the orders it ranks on order value and states no rupiah total.

## Bram, Execution

`execution` · effort `medium`

Writes back to SAP within its authority, tells the affected roles, drafts the notice to a customer whose order is late and the request to a supplier for a new date (both wait for a person to send), watches through to receipt, and records what actually happened so the next incident can learn from this one.

### Tools (9/9)

- **`create_stock_transfer`**(material, from_plant, to_plant, qty, cost_idr)
  <br>Move stock between plants. Autonomous only when the cost impact is under Rp 50 million AND every figure the decision rests on is trusted. A modelled or missing figure anywhere under it sends this to a person however cheap it is.
- **`create_draft_po`**(supplier, material, qty, needed_by, cost_idr)
  <br>Create a DRAFT purchase order. Always waits for buyer approval, never autonomous.
- **`notify`**(role, message)
  <br>Tell an affected role inside the company (planner, buyer, procurement_lead, qa), with the evidence that backs it. Internal only: a customer or a supplier is told through draft_customer_notice or request_supplier_update.
- **`draft_customer_notice`**(sales_order, message, new_date?)
  <br>Draft a notice to a customer that their order will be late, or short, and what is being done about it. The customer and their email address are read from the order and the business partner record, never typed in. A DRAFT: it always waits for a procurement lead to approve it, whatever it says; once approved SIGAP emails it exactly as written, so write it as the finished message.
- **`request_supplier_update`**(purchase_order, question)
  <br>Draft a request to a supplier about one of its purchase orders: confirm the date, give a revised one, say what has shipped. The supplier and its email address are read from the order and the business partner record, never typed in. A DRAFT: it waits for a buyer to approve it; once approved SIGAP emails it exactly as written, so write it as the finished message. The answer, once it comes, is recorded with record_outcome.
- **`monitor_shipment`**(reference)
  <br>File a watch on a shipment so its outcome is owed and asked for. SIGAP does not poll the carrier: this records that this reference still needs a result, and a person, or you, closes it with record_outcome once receipt is actually known. Nothing here observes an arrival, so nothing here can invent one.
- **`record_outcome`**(action_id, outcome, detail?, actual_cost_idr?)
  <br>Close the loop on an action that was sent: what actually happened, and when. Writes it to the record so the next incident can learn from this one.
- **`create_sourcing_event`**(material, rationale)
  <br>Open a sourcing event to qualify a second supplier for a material. A DRAFT for procurement to approve. Never autonomous, whatever the value.
- **`propose_safety_stock_change`**(material, plant, new_level, rationale)
  <br>Propose a new safety stock floor for a material at a plant. This is a policy change rather than a transaction, so it always escalates. It is never sent to SAP by the sender; a person applies it.

## Vega, Risk Scanner

`exposure` · effort `medium`

Prevent mode: hunts risk that has not happened yet. Single sources, concentrated lanes, certificates about to lapse, and natural hazards closing in on a port or plant we depend on.

### Tools (4/4)

- **`scan_supply_exposure`**(horizon_weeks?, cover_floor_days?)
  <br>Scan for risk that has not happened yet: materials with a single source, lanes concentrated on one port, and cover falling below policy inside the horizon. Ranked by the committed customer value behind each material. Deterministic.
- **`get_supplier_certifications`**(supplier)
  <br>A supplier's TKDN certificates from the rule pack: the certified percentage, the validity dates and the days left before each lapses. A certificate that lapses drops the local-content ratio with no physical event at all.
- **`rank_po_slip_risk`**(top?)
  <br>Rank purchase orders that have not shipped yet by how many days they are likely to slip, predicted by SAP RPT-1.6 from the in-transit orders whose slip is already known. Returns each order's predicted slip and RPT's interval. A ranking signal, labelled modelled: use it to decide what to chase first, never as a date or a cost.
- **`scan_live_hazards`**(no arguments)
  <br>Every natural hazard happening now within reach of a place the supply chain touches: our plants, and the loading port of every open purchase order. Cyclones, floods, quakes, eruptions, wildfires and droughts from GDACS (graded Green, Orange or Red), NASA EONET and USGS, one storm reported by two feeds counted once, each with its distance to the place and the radius it is judged by. Live positions, measured against the profile and order book.
